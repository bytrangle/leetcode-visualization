LeetCode Practice Visualizer

A web app that replays your LeetCode practice history as an animated, difficulty-colored heatmap.

status · approved working title — not yet named

The Unique Kernel

01

It's a replay, not a picture

The GitHub contribution graph is static. This unfolds a history chronologically — day by day through a year, then year after year until the history runs out, each day filling in as the replay reaches it.

Strip the animation and you're left with a static heatmap that already exists in the world.

02

Color answers a different question

GitHub's grid says how much did you do. This says how hard was what you did — each day weighted by the average difficulty of the problems solved in it, not by their count.

Strip the difficulty weighting and you're left with GitHub's grid, for LeetCode.

What “Working” Looks Like

STEP 1 / 8

Landing page

A title, a welcome message, and an input for the visitor's LeetCode username.

STEP 2 / 8

Welcome message clears

On submit, it disappears and a heatmap of 365 cells appears — every cell light grey.

STEP 3 / 8

The year is chosen

The displayed year is the earliest year with practice data for that username. The label sits above the grid.

STEP 4 / 8

The replay begins

It starts at that year's earliest day with practice data and fills the cell with the color for that day's average difficulty.

STEP 5 / 8

The cube flip

The cell transitions from light grey to its final color — the learner's words: “it will look like someone flips a cube.”

STEP 6 / 8

Through to the year's end

It continues, day by day, to the last day of that year that has data. Days with no practice stay grey and are skipped.

STEP 7 / 8

Next year

Every cell resets to light grey and the process repeats for the next year with data.

STEP 8 / 8

The finish line

It repeats until the final year with data. That whole run is the proof — and it has to be showable in about a minute.

The Boundary

In  The POC boundary

Exactly the flow above, and nothing else. Asked what to cut, the learner's answer was that everything described is the whole thing.

That makes this an unusually tight scope. Arriving at a precise, demonstrable, single-loop proof of concept is normally the hard work of this conversation — and it was already done before we started.

Every element is load-bearing: the replay is the kernel; difficulty-colored cells are the other half of it; the popup listing that day's problems is what makes the color legible; and hover to re-show a past popup is how a visitor inspects anything once the replay has moved on.

Submission needs a demo video and a public GitHub repository. Deployment is optional and replaces neither.

Later  Worth doing, not now

None named by the learner.

Cut  Explicitly removed

None — and that's a finding rather than an omission.

Nothing was cut because nothing beyond the described flow was proposed. The proof of concept is small enough that no proposed feature needed removing to protect it.

Two things follow. The pressure to add will arrive during 5-build, not now, and it will feel reasonable at the time — this section is the record that it wasn't part of the plan. And several behaviors a visitor expects were never proposed at all: those are unaddressed, not cut.

Notes — observed, not decided

  • Data source. leetcode-api-pied.vercel.app/user/{username}/calendar — a third-party, unofficially-hosted API, not one run by LeetCode. No SLA; it may change or vanish. The learner's position: “we may have to use other APIs if necessary.” 4-spec treats this as a known dependency risk and designs a fallback.
  • Playback control was never raised. Pause, restart, speed, skip-a-year — undiscussed. 3-prd should ask rather than assume either way.
  • No-data and error behavior was never raised. What a visitor sees for a mistyped username, an account with no solves, or a failed API call is undefined.
  • Popup timing is approximate. “2 seconds or something” — a placeholder, not a decision.
  • Name unset. The project has no name yet; 3-prd uses the learner's chosen name.
  • Who It's For is a category, not a person. “LeetCode users who have solved problems” is a direction, not a picture. 3-prd can sharpen it.
